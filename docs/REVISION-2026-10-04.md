This is the estate's cleanest spec-to-code loop (generated from the
codebase, claims exercised by real output). The product is where I'd
push hardest:

1. **The leaderboard is a Goodhart engine by construction.** §7.2
   ranks by count DESC, tie-break earliest-max-log. "Count DESC"
   on a habit tracker with a public board means the optimal
   strategy is many cheap logs, and the tie-break rewards logging
   early, not logging consistently. From scratch: rank by
   *streak integrity* (current streak × log regularity, with a
   cap on logs/day so a user can't out-log a week in an hour), or
   explicitly state "this board rewards volume and that is the
   point" — an acknowledged Goodhart beats an accidental one.
   The spec currently doesn't notice the question exists.
2. **The spec's honesty about its own subject (the content note)
   is the right pattern** — keep it, and extend it to the leaderboard:
   a one-line "this mechanic can be gamed by X; we accept that
   because Y" under every ranked surface.
3. **Streak calculation (§7.1) is the core business logic.** It
   has the detail; what it lacks is the edge-case table (timezone
   boundaries, DST, missed-day grace, what happens at streak reset).
   From scratch: the edge-case table IS the spec section, because
   that's where streak trackers diverge.
4. **36 commits, last activity 2026-09-30.** The spec says
   "authoritative, generated from the actual codebase" — generation
   is the right mechanism precisely because it self-invalidates
   when the code moves. Keep the regeneration as a gate (a
   CI step that fails when SPECIFICATION.md hash ≠ regenerated
   output), so "authoritative" stays true without a human
   remembering.
5. **The manifesto/drill pages are content, not product.** They're
   documented as subsystems (§12/§13). From scratch: content lives
   in data (seeded rows), spec'd as "admin-editable curated text
   collections" — one paragraph — not as two subsystems. The
   tracker is the product.
