Use aliases. Vet people before sharing locations or personal details. Have a simple code of conduct and a way to report problems. Everything stays adult, consensual, and legal. No one is forcing anyone.

Track real metrics: number of consistent meetup spots, active members who show up more than once, growth in the private groups, feedback on what actually changed someone's habits. Ignore fantasy scoreboards.

This grows the same way most subcultures do—small groups doing the boring organizational work repeatedly. Use the tools that already exist, keep the focus on real connections and beta roles where they fit, and avoid turning every post into performance. The rest follows from consistent, low-drama action.
