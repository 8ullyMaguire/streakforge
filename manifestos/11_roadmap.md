## What this site is for

StreakForge is a counter, a lock, and a board. You log once a day. The number goes up, or it resets. Everybody can see where they stand.

That is the whole product, and it works. What follows is where it goes next.

## The first thing missing: someone else holding the key

Right now the only person who can end your lock is you. That makes the lock a promise to yourself, and a promise to yourself is worth exactly as much as you want it to be on a bad afternoon.

The fix is small. Name a keyholder — another user, or an account your partner controls. While you are locked, ending it takes their approval, not yours. You request; they decide.

This changes what the site is. A keyholder who logs in and sees three people waiting on them is a reason to come back that no streak counter can match. And it means the site is somewhere you *bring* someone, rather than a thing you use alone.

The rule that matters: a keyholder who goes quiet must not lock you out forever. After N days without a response, it falls back to you, and the site says so plainly. Anything else is a trap, not a kink.

## Proof that survives a screenshot

A picture of a counter saying fourteen days is worth nothing. Everyone can edit a picture.

So make the claim checkable. Every lock carries a proof key: a short string derived from when you locked, who you are, and a secret the server holds. It is on your profile. Anyone can paste it in and find out whether it is real.

It is a small amount of work and it changes what a boast is worth. "Bet you can't stay locked longer" stops being a thing you say and becomes a thing that can be settled.

Which needs the second half: a public record of when you logged. Dates and kinds, no notes, no private text. If your history is legible, a proof key is worth checking.

## Cards worth posting

The single most useful thing you could do with this site is leave it.

One tap makes an image: your name, how long you have been locked, today's score, where you rank, in the colours you already recognise. Post it to Bluesky, post it to X. Every active user becomes a billboard, and the people who see it are already the right audience.

Post to Bluesky first. It is where this crowd already is, and there is a feed for it.

## Talking to you when you are not here

A streak product that only speaks to you when you open the tab is a diary.

The site already knows exactly when your day ends — the countdown to midnight UTC is right there on the dashboard. One message a day, at the time you already committed to, saying the same thing the button would say. Opt in, once a day, no nagging.

Also: install it. The manifest is already in place, but nothing is actually installable yet, so an app icon on your home screen — one tap, opens instantly, works on a bad connection — is worth more than it sounds.

## Seasons

Locktober. No Nut November. The guides in this library already talk about both, so the interest is there before the site does anything.

A named period with its own board and a badge for people who finished it. The lock table is already the right shape for it.

One catch to decide first: scores now reset after thirty days of silence, so an event longer than a month would wipe a score halfway through. Either events are exempt, or they run for a month. Worth settling before, not after.

## The library is the front door

The texts in this library are the best thing the site has, and right now none of them are findable by anyone who has not already found the site.

Give each one its own page, with a real title, and chain them — start with the starting guide, end up somewhere you did not expect. It is the one channel that brings people in without anyone having to post about it.

## Two things to fix before sharing the link widely

Not features. Just things that will bite.

The public feed hands out the note field — up to 140 characters of whatever you typed — to anyone who loads the page, signed in or not. Say one useful thing there by accident and it is public. There should be a switch.

And the feed is one column-signature change away from breaking the way the leaderboard broke twice this month. It should not be that fragile.

## What will not change

No adverts. The dark, the red, the gold — that is the look, and the look is the brand.

No email, no phone number, no proving who you are. The anonymity is the appeal.

No paywall on the counter or the board. Those stay free or nobody has a reason to come back.

And the board keeps its meaning: the top of it is the people denying hardest. Anything that made it reward the opposite would not be an improvement.
